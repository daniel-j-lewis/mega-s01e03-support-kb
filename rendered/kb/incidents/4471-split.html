Each report is counted once (the export repeats some), in exactly one row. The rows are worked out from the tenant, not from intake’s tag: intake guesses from the reporter’s browser, and the browser is not where the date was typed.

parser: day-first dates read month first

Every tenant on file writes the day first. The shared parser reads month first, so day and month swap whenever the day is 12 or less: 7 April goes out as 4 July. This is the bug #4471 names, and the fix that is queued.

hint: the booking-v3 form asks for MM/DD/YYYY

booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People at day-first tenants type what the hint says, and the form reads it the tenant’s way. The date is swapped before any parser sees it, so the parser fix does not help. Fix the hint (or use a date picker). These reports came in through the form at booking-v3 tenants.

one-day: dates move by one day

Some dates land a day early instead of swapped: Tuesday 12 May shows as Monday 11 May, one meeting is Tuesday in Lisbon and Monday in Toronto, an invite arrives before its booking. Date order cannot do that. Look at where a booking’s date crosses time zones. The desk already marked these as one-day moves; the #4471 page counts them under parser and only footnotes them.

Why they were merged

Triage merges on the subject line alone (decision 0001). Bugs that all feel like “wrong date” share subject lines, so they merged into one ticket. Read the body and the tenant before merging into a ticket that has a fix queued. Changing the rule needs a new decision record, not an edit to 0001.

Lessons

  • A big ticket can be several bugs that sound the same. Count by cause, not by subject.
  • Check what a fix covers before marking a ticket known.
